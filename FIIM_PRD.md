## 1. Product Vision

To become the leading evidence-based athlete monitoring platform that transforms how sports organizations predict, prevent, and manage fatigue-related injuries—shifting the global paradigm from reactive injury treatment to proactive injury prevention through intelligent data insights.

We envision a future where every coach, sport scientist, and medical professional has real-time, actionable intelligence to keep athletes healthy, optimize performance, and extend athletic careers.

## 2. Product Overview

**Fatigue Injury Index Monitoring (FIIM)** is a web-based athlete monitoring platform designed to aggregate, analyze, and visualize critical sport science indicators related to fatigue, training load, recovery status, and injury risk. 

FIIM serves as a centralized intelligence hub for multi-disciplinary sports teams, enabling coaches, sport scientists, physiotherapists, sports doctors, and organizational administrators to make informed, data-driven decisions that protect athlete health and optimize training outcomes.

Built on evidence-based sport science methodologies, FIIM translates complex physiological and biomechanical data into intuitive dashboards, alerts, and risk indices that are accessible to both technical and non-technical users. The platform's core philosophy is **prevention over treatment**—identifying injury risk before it manifests into lost training time, competition absence, or career-threatening conditions.

## 3. Problem Statement

Sports organizations at all levels—from elite professional clubs to university programs and youth academies—face a critical and costly challenge: **athlete injuries caused by unmanaged fatigue, excessive training load, and inadequate recovery.**

### Key Pain Points:

- **Reactive, Not Proactive:** Most existing solutions and organizational workflows focus on injury management *after* occurrence, resulting in lost athlete availability, compromised team performance, and increased medical costs.

- **Data Fragmentation:** Critical athlete health data (training loads, subjective wellness scores, sleep quality, biomechanical metrics) is often collected in silos using spreadsheets, paper forms, or disconnected tools—making holistic risk assessment impossible.

- **Lack of Accessible Insights:** While sport science research has identified validated fatigue and injury risk indicators, these insights rarely reach practitioners in actionable, real-time formats. Coaches and medical staff lack the tools to operationalize evidence-based monitoring at scale.

- **Inconsistent Monitoring:** Without standardized monitoring protocols and centralized visibility, high-risk athletes slip through the cracks, and intervention opportunities are missed.

- **High Cost of Injuries:** Fatigue-related injuries cost professional clubs millions in medical expenses, lost wages, and diminished competitive results. For universities and academies, injuries derail athlete development and scholarship investments.

**In short:** The sports industry lacks an integrated, user-friendly, and scientifically grounded platform to proactively monitor fatigue and injury risk across entire athlete populations.

## 4. Product Goals

### Primary Goals:

1. **Enable Proactive Injury Prevention:** Deliver a platform that identifies elevated fatigue and injury risk levels *before* injuries occur, allowing timely intervention by coaches and medical staff.

2. **Centralize Athlete Monitoring Data:** Unify training load, recovery, wellness, and medical data into a single, accessible web application—eliminating fragmented workflows and data silos.

3. **Operationalize Sport Science:** Translate peer-reviewed, evidence-based monitoring indicators (e.g., acute:chronic workload ratio, heart rate variability, RPE, sleep quality) into automated, easy-to-understand risk scores and dashboards.

4. **Empower Multi-Disciplinary Teams:** Provide tailored views and workflows for coaches, sport scientists, physiotherapists, doctors, and administrators so each stakeholder can act on relevant insights.

5. **Support Scalable Adoption:** Design the product to serve professional clubs, universities, sports academies, and clinical practices with flexible configuration, user management, and pricing.

### Secondary Goals:

- Establish FIIM as a credible, research-backed brand in sports technology and sport science SaaS.
- Build a data foundation that supports future predictive modeling and machine learning capabilities.
- Ensure compliance with health data privacy regulations (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA) to enable global deployment.

### 9.5 User Pain Points

#### Athlete Pain Points
- **Survey Fatigue:** Repetitive, lengthy wellness questionnaires that feel pointless because they never see feedback.
- **Distrust:** Fear that readiness data will be used punitively (e.g., contract negotiations, benching) rather than protectively.
- **Ambiguity:** Receiving raw numbers (e.g., "HRV is 42 ms") with no interpretive context about what to do differently today.
- **Privacy Concerns:** Unclear who can see what, especially in teams with porous boundaries between coaches and medical staff.

#### Coach Pain Points
- **Information Overload:** Presented with too many disconnected metrics (GPS, RPE, sleep, HRV) and no unified risk signal.
- **Tool Fatigue:** Already using film software, scheduling apps, and messaging platforms; will reject another login unless it is indispensable.
- **Credibility Gap:** Distrusts wellness tools that cannot explain their logic or contradict obvious on-field observations.
- **Time Pressure:** Morning practice planning happens fast; dashboards must be scannable in under 60 seconds.

#### Sport Scientist Pain Points
- **Manual Data Wrangling:** Spending hours copying CSV exports into Excel, cleaning data, and calculating rolling averages.
- **Black-Box Algorithms:** Commercial platforms that generate "risk scores" without revealing formulas, making internal validation impossible.
- **Poor Data Integrity:** Athletes forgetting to fill surveys, wearables not syncing, or coaches manually entering conflicting session data.
- **Reporting Burden:** Expected to produce weekly PDFs for management using tools ill-suited for repeatable, automated reporting.

#### Physiotherapist Pain Points
- **Reactive Firefighting:** Most of the day is spent treating injuries that have already occurred rather than preventing new ones.
- **Siloed Records:** Treatment notes in one system, training loads in another, and subjective wellness in a third.
- **Communication Gaps:** Coaches making lineup decisions without seeing updated return-to-play status or clearance stages.
- **Alert Blindness:** Risk notifications buried in email inboxes or generic dashboards that are not designed for mobile clinical workflows.

#### Sports Doctor Pain Points
- **Fragmented Medical History:** Difficult to view long-term load, wellness, and injury recurrence patterns in a single longitudinal record.
- **Platform Distrust:** Concerned that monitoring platforms conflate wellness data with clinical diagnosis, creating liability.
- **Access Control Anxiety:** Need airtight separation between medical notes and coaching-accessible monitoring data.
- **Administrative Load:** Spending excessive time writing manual reports for compliance, insurance, or executive review.

#### Club Administrator Pain Points
- **Provisioning Hell:** Adding a new team or department currently requires emails, tickets, and manual configuration.
- **Access Chaos:** Uncertainty about who can see sensitive athlete data, especially when staff turnover is high.
- **Compliance Uncertainty:** Difficulty demonstrating GDPR or HIPAA adherence without built-in audit trails and consent management.
- **ROI Ambiguity:** Lack of built-in reporting to prove platform adoption, data completeness, or injury reduction to finance departments.

### 9.7 Key Insights from User Analysis

1. **Trust is the currency of adoption.** Athletes must trust that data protects them. Coaches must trust the risk signal. Scientists must trust the methodology. Doctors must trust clinical boundaries.

2. **Time is the scarcest resource.** Every primary user operates under severe time pressure. FIIM must deliver actionable intelligence in seconds, not minutes.

3. **Roles must be rigidly separated.** Medical privacy, coaching authority, and scientific governance require granular, enforceable access controls that feel seamless, not bureaucratic.

4. **Feedback loops close engagement.** Athletes complete surveys when they see immediate, meaningful feedback. Coaches use dashboards when decisions improve. Scientists invest when reporting becomes effortless.

5. **Proactive intervention is the shared win condition.** The entire user ecosystem—athlete, coach, scientist, physio, doctor, administrator—benefits when a preventable injury is avoided. FIIM's UX must make that win visible and repeatable.

## 10. User Roles and Permissions

### 10.1 Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) Framework Overview

FIIM implements a strict, hierarchical Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) model designed to enforce data privacy, clinical governance, and operational security across multi-disciplinary sports organizations. Every user is assigned exactly one primary role per organization. Permissions are additive at the role level and restrictive at the data level—users can only interact with modules, actions, and athlete records explicitly granted to their role.

The RBAC model is built on four core principles:

1. **Principle of Least Privilege:** Users receive the minimum access necessary to fulfill their professional responsibilities.
2. **Medical Data Segregation:** Clinical notes, diagnoses, and sensitive medical records are walled off from coaching and administrative roles.
3. **Auditability:** Every data view, export, edit, and configuration change is logged with user identity, timestamp, and IP address.
4. **Contextual Scoping:** Access is further scoped by team, squad, or individual athlete assignment. A coach for the U18 squad cannot view first-team medical data unless explicitly authorized.

### 10.9 RBAC Governance & Enforcement

1. **Role Assignment:** Only Administrators can assign or change roles. Self-elevation is impossible. Role changes trigger an immediate email notification to the affected user and their department head.

2. **Contextual Scoping:** All roles (except Administrator and Sports Doctor) are scoped by team, squad, or caseload assignment. A user with Coach role for the U18 team cannot view first-team data unless a separate assignment record exists.

3. **Time-Bound Access:** Temporary access elevations (e.g., covering for an absent physio) can be granted by Administrators with an explicit expiration date. Elevated access is flagged in the UI and fully audited.

4. **Medical Firewall:** The medical data layer (clinical notes, diagnoses, prescriptions, imaging references) is protected by an additional permission bit: `MEDICAL_READ` or `MEDICAL_WRITE`. Possessing the Physiotherapist or Sports Doctor role automatically grants these bits, but they can be revoked independently by a Sports Doctor or Administrator in cases of staff disciplinary action.

5. **Data Minimization for Athletes:** Athletes are physically incapable of viewing peer data at the API level. Team averages are computed server-side and never exposed to the athlete client unless the organization explicitly enables a "team benchmark" feature with athlete opt-in consent.

6. **Immutable Audit Trail:** Every permission check, data view, export, and configuration change is written to an append-only audit log stored separately from application databases. Retention aligns with GDPR (5 years standard, configurable by jurisdiction) and is non-deletable by application users.

### 11.5 ACWR Monitoring

**Purpose**

**Business Objectives**

- Provide real-time, individualized traffic-light risk classification for coaching decisions.
- Reduce soft-tissue injury incidence by flagging dangerous load spikes before tissue adaptation fails.
- Support sport scientist credibility with transparent, configurable calculation parameters.

**Inputs**
- Daily total load values (internal sRPE or external player load/GPS-derived metrics).
- Acute window definition (default: 7 days; configurable).

**Outputs**
- Daily ACWR ratio per athlete per load metric.

- Trend arrows and rolling trajectory over past 14 days.
- Population summary: number of athletes in each risk zone.
- Alert triggers when athletes cross from optimal to high or very high zones.

**Main Features**
- Configurable acute and chronic windows with sport-scientist override.
- Support for both rolling average and EWMA calculation methods.
- Multiple parallel ACWR tracks (e.g., sRPE-based, distance-based, high-speed-distance-based).
- Automatic exclusion rules for non-training days (e.g., rest days, injury days).

- Methodology documentation panel explaining formulas in plain language for coaches.

**Success Criteria**

- ACWR risk zone classification accuracy validated against published research thresholds.

- Soft-tissue injury rate in athletes with sustained ACWR > 1.50 is demonstrably higher than in optimal-zone athletes (internal validation).
- Coach understanding of ACWR signal validated by quarterly survey > 80% comprehension.

### 11.6 Monotony and Strain

**Purpose**
Detect harmful training patterns characterized by repetitive, unvaried load (Monotony) and the product of high load with high monotony (Strain). Complement ACWR by capturing the qualitative structure of training weeks, not just the magnitude ratio.

**Business Objectives**
- Identify psychologically and physiologically stressful microcycles that increase illness and overtraining risk.
- Encourage load variation and recovery day integration in periodization.
- Provide sport scientists with an additional validated risk dimension beyond simple load accumulation.
- Reduce non-contact injury and illness incidence linked to under-recovery and repetitive stress.

**Inputs**
- Daily load totals (same source as ACWR: sRPE or external load metrics).
- Weekly load series (7-day blocks).
- Training day classification (full, partial, rest, travel, competition).
- Athlete wellness and illness data (for outcome correlation).

**Outputs**
- Weekly Monotony index: mean daily load / standard deviation of daily load.
- Weekly Strain index: total weekly load x Monotony.
- Risk classification based on published thresholds (e.g., Strain > 4,000 AU = elevated risk).
- Historical Monotony and Strain trend lines per athlete.
- Population-level overreaching risk summary.

**Main Features**
- Automatic weekly Monotony and Strain calculation after each training day.
- Strain threshold alerts configurable by sport scientists.
- Visual comparison of load distribution across the 7-day microcycle (bar chart with rest-day gaps).
- Overlay of Monotony/Strain peaks with subsequent illness or injury events.
- Scenario modeling: sport scientists can simulate planned week structures to preview projected Strain.
- Integration with wellness module to flag weeks where high Strain coincides with poor recovery scores.

**Success Criteria**
- Monotony and Strain recalculated automatically within 30 minutes of daily load finalization.
- Strain alert specificity: > 60% of Strain-alerted weeks are followed by illness, injury, or wellness drop within 7 days.
- Periodization change adoption: teams using FIIM Monotony feedback show measurable increase in rest-day inclusion and load variance within one season.
- Zero manual calculation required by sport scientists; all outputs automated from Training Load Monitoring inputs.

## 12. Sport Science Framework

The Sport Science Framework defines the evidence-based algorithms, composite indices, and analytical logic that power FIIM's decision-support engine. Every index is grounded in peer-reviewed sport science, designed for transparency, and configurable by qualified sport scientists to match organizational context, sport demands, and available data streams.

All indices are computed server-side with full audit trails. Formulas, weightings, and version histories are accessible to Sport Scientist and Sports Doctor roles. Coaches and athletes receive interpreted outputs; raw calculation parameters are exposed on demand to maintain trust and scientific rigor.

---

### 12.1 Fatigue Index

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Quantify accumulated physiological and psychological fatigue to identify athletes approaching a performance decrement or non-functional overreaching state that elevates injury risk. |
| **Variables** | Subjective fatigue rating (1–10); subjective muscle soreness (1–10); sleep quality deficit vs. individual baseline; 7-day cumulative sRPE or external load; heart rate variability (HRV) trend deviation (if available). |
| **Calculation Logic** | Each variable is normalized to a 0–100 scale using the athlete's 28-day individual mean and standard deviation. **Fatigue Index = (0.30 × Normalized Fatigue) + (0.25 × Normalized Soreness) + (0.20 × Sleep Deficit Penalty) + (0.15 × 7-Day Load Percentile) + (0.10 × HRV Trend Penalty)**. HRV penalty is omitted if no wearable data is present; weights redistribute proportionally. |
| **Interpretation** | **0–30:** Low fatigue — full training capacity. **30–50:** Moderate fatigue — standard monitoring, no modification required. **50–75:** High fatigue — consider load reduction or recovery intervention. **75–100:** Critical fatigue — high probability of overreaching; mandatory review by sport scientist and medical staff. |
| **Practical Use** | Flag athletes for reduced training volume, individual recovery protocols, or targeted manual therapy. Triggers internal discussion between coach and sport scientist when an athlete crosses the high threshold. |
| **Required Data** | Daily wellness survey (fatigue, soreness, sleep quality); daily session RPE or external load; optional wearable-derived HRV or sleep metrics. Minimum requirement: wellness survey + load data. |
| **Decision Making Impact** | Coaches reduce individual drill exposure for high-fatigue athletes. Physiotherapists prioritize treatment-room allocation. Sport scientists review microcycle structure to identify causative load patterns. |

---

### 12.2 Wellness Index

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Aggregate subjective self-reported wellness dimensions into a single daily health and recovery signal that captures subclinical deviations in an athlete's perceived readiness before objective performance markers change. |
| **Variables** | Fatigue (1–5), mood (1–5), stress (1–5), sleep quality (1–5), sleep duration (hours), muscle soreness (1–5), illness symptoms (binary or 1–5). Optional custom dimensions: menstrual cycle symptom scale, travel fatigue, hydration. |
| **Calculation Logic** | Each dimension is converted to a 0–100 score. **Wellness Index = Σ (Dimension Score × Dimension Weight)**. Default equal weighting; sport scientists may adjust weights. A **Wellness Deviation** score is also calculated: z-score of today's index vs. the athlete's 28-day rolling mean. Flag thresholds: deviation < −1.0 = notable decline; < −1.5 = significant decline. |
| **Interpretation** | **> 80:** Excellent wellness — full capacity. **60–80:** Good wellness — minor fluctuations, standard training. **40–60:** Compromised — one or more dimensions elevated; follow-up recommended. **< 40:** Poor wellness — high probability of illness, fatigue, or psychosocial stress; intervention warranted. |
| **Practical Use** | Morning triage tool. Coaches and sport scientists review the team wellness grid at the start of each day. Athletes with compromised or poor scores receive a check-in from medical or support staff before training. |
| **Required Data** | Daily wellness questionnaire responses. Minimum: fatigue, sleep quality, soreness. Complete battery recommended for maximum validity. |
| **Decision Making Impact** | Influences session prescription (reduce intensity for poor wellness). Triggers medical screening for illness symptoms. Contributes directly to Fatigue Index, Readiness Index, and Injury Risk Index. |

---

### 12.3 Workload Index

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Summarize total training and competitive load exposure over defined periods to establish a ground-truth volume metric for periodization tracking, load-ratio modeling, and inter-athlete comparison. |
| **Variables** | **Internal load:** Session RPE (sRPE) = session duration (minutes) × average session RPE (1–10 or 6–20 Borg). **External load:** Total distance (m), high-speed running distance (> 5.5 m/s), sprint distance (> 7.0 m/s), PlayerLoad (arbitrary units from accelerometer), number of accelerations/decelerations > 2.5 m/s², time in heart rate zones. |
| **Calculation Logic** | Daily total per metric = Σ(session values). Weekly Workload Index = sum of daily totals over 7 days. Rolling averages calculated over 7, 14, 21, and 28 days. Planned vs. actual variance = ((Actual − Planned) / Planned) × 100. Percentile rank within position group and squad computed daily. |
| **Interpretation** | Absolute values are tracked against individual historical baselines, position-specific norms, and periodization targets. **Underload:** < 80% of planned weekly load or < 0.80 ACWR. **Target:** 80–100% of planned load. **Overload:** > 110% of planned load or ACWR > 1.30. |
| **Practical Use** | Compare planned versus actual weekly microcycles. Identify athletes who are underloaded (detraining risk) or overloaded (spike risk). Feed rolling averages into ACWR, Monotony, and Strain modules automatically. |
| **Required Data** | Session duration and RPE (from athlete or coach); or GPS/wearable-derived external metrics via API or file upload. Minimum: one load metric per session (internal or external). |
| **Decision Making Impact** | Sport scientists adjust next week's microcycle to close planned-actual gaps. Coaches identify athletes needing additional exposure or forced rest. Direct input for ACWR, Monotony, Strain, and Injury Risk Index calculations. |

---

### 12.4 Recovery Index

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Evaluate an athlete's recuperative status based on sleep and autonomic recovery markers to determine readiness for high-intensity neuromuscular and metabolic work. |
| **Variables** | Sleep quality (subjective 1–5); sleep duration (hours) vs. individual need (e.g., 8 hours); sleep efficiency (% deep/REM from wearable, optional); resting HRV (morning RMSSD or SDNN, optional); subjective recovery rating (1–10); rest day status (binary). |
| **Calculation Logic** | **Sleep Score = (0.60 × Sleep Duration Score + 0.40 × Sleep Quality Score)**. Duration score = min(100, (actual hours / target hours) × 100). If HRV available: **HRV Score = 100 − |z-score of RMSSD vs. 28-day baseline| × 20**. **Recovery Index = (0.40 × Sleep Score + 0.30 × HRV Score + 0.20 × Subjective Recovery Score + 0.10 × Rest Day Bonus)**. If HRV unavailable, weights redistribute to sleep and subjective recovery. |
| **Interpretation** | **> 85:** Fully recovered — cleared for high-intensity and high-speed work. **70–85:** Adequate recovery — standard training prescription appropriate. **55–70:** Incomplete recovery — consider reduction in high-speed or power work. **< 55:** Poor recovery — prioritize sleep hygiene, active recovery, or medical review; withhold high-intensity work. |
| **Practical Use** | Guide session intensity modulation. Athletes with poor recovery are withheld from sprint and plyometric work even if load metrics are within normal ranges. Sleep education and intervention triggered for chronically poor scores. |
| **Required Data** | Daily sleep survey (duration, quality); optional wearable sleep/HRV sync; optional subjective recovery question. Minimum: sleep duration + quality. |
| **Decision Making Impact** | Coaches substitute high-intensity drills for low-intensity alternatives for poor-recovery athletes. Sport scientists review training block structure for insufficient recovery days. Medical staff screen for sleep disorders or illness in chronically low scores. |

---

### 12.5 Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR)

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Compare recent workload to an established chronic baseline to identify dangerous load spikes that are strongly associated with soft-tissue injury, particularly in the lower limb and hamstring. |
| **Variables** | Daily total load (sRPE or selected external metric); acute window length (default 7 days); chronic window length (default 28 days); calculation method (rolling average or exponentially weighted moving average — EWMA). |
| **Calculation Logic** | **Rolling Average Method:** ACWR = (Sum of daily load over acute days) / (Sum of daily load over chronic days / (chronic days / acute days)). **EWMA Method:** ACWR = Acute EWMA / Chronic EWMA, where EWMA_t = λ × Load_t + (1 − λ) × EWMA_{t−1}; λ_acute = 2 / (acute_days + 1), λ_chronic = 2 / (chronic_days + 1). Sport scientists may toggle between methods. |
| **Interpretation** | **< 0.80:** Underload — detraining risk, tissue resilience may decline. **0.80–1.30:** Optimal — safe adaptation zone, lowest relative injury risk. **1.30–1.50:** High risk — elevated soft-tissue injury probability; load reduction or modified participation advised. **> 1.50:** Very high risk — acute spike significantly exceeds tissue tolerance; high probability of injury within 7–14 days. |
| **Practical Use** | Daily pre-session check by coaches and sport scientists. Athletes in high/very-high zones are candidates for load reduction, modified drills, or rest regardless of subjective wellness. |
| **Required Data** | Daily load totals for the selected metric (sRPE or external). Minimum 8 days of historical data required for first valid ACWR calculation (7 acute + 1 prior day for chronic initialization). |
| **Decision Making Impact** | Primary input for go/no-go and modified-participation decisions. Sport scientists adjust chronic baseline targets and periodization plans. Coaches substitute or rest high-ACWR athletes. Medical staff prioritize screening for athletes with sustained ratios > 1.50. |

---

### 12.6 Monotony

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Quantify the day-to-day variability of training load within a microcycle. Low variability (high monotony) is associated with psychological staleness, suppressed immune function, and overtraining syndrome. |
| **Variables** | Daily total load values (sRPE or external metric) across a 7-day microcycle; non-training day handling rule (include as zero or exclude from calculation — configurable by sport scientist). |
| **Calculation Logic** | **Monotony = Mean Daily Load / Standard Deviation of Daily Load**. If standard deviation = 0 (all identical loads or all rest days), the system returns a null value and flags the week as either "complete rest" or "maximum monotony — review required." Non-training days may be excluded if the sport scientist configures the protocol to assess load-day variability only. |
| **Interpretation** | **< 2.0:** Variable training week — good load fluctuation with recovery days integrated. **2.0–2.5:** Moderate monotony — acceptable for certain training phases (e.g., pre-competition taper), but monitor wellness closely. **> 2.5:** High monotony — repetitive loading pattern; strong association with illness, overreaching, and mood disturbance. Immediate periodization review recommended. |
| **Practical Use** | Evaluate microcycle design quality. Flag weeks where recovery days are missing or where session intensities are undifferentiated. Encourage sport scientists and coaches to build variance into the weekly plan. |
| **Required Data** | 7 consecutive days of daily load totals. Partial weeks (e.g., mid-week calculation) produce provisional values with confidence flags. |
| **Decision Making Impact** | Sport scientists redesign upcoming microcycles to increase session-type variance. Coaches adjust session structure to break up repetitive high-intensity blocks. Medical staff anticipate immune-related illness in teams with sustained high monotony. |

---

### 12.7 Strain

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Capture the overall physiological and psychological stress of a training microcycle by combining total volume with monotony. High strain represents a perfect storm of high load and low variability — the most dangerous combination for overtraining and illness. |
| **Variables** | Weekly total load (7-day sum of sRPE or external metric); weekly Monotony index. |
| **Calculation Logic** | **Strain = Weekly Total Load × Monotony**. Expressed in arbitrary units (AU). Example: weekly load of 3,500 AU with monotony of 2.2 = Strain of 7,700 AU. If Monotony is null (e.g., complete rest week), Strain is also null with explanatory annotation. |
| **Interpretation** | Relative to individual and team historical distribution. Sport scientists set percentile-based or absolute thresholds. **Typical benchmarks (sRPE-based):** < 3,000 AU low; 3,000–4,000 AU moderate; > 4,000 AU high strain. **Critical threshold:** sustained strain > 5,000 AU strongly associated with illness and non-functional overreaching. Interpretation must be sport- and metric-specific. |
| **Practical Use** | Identify psychologically and physiologically stressful microcycles that demand immediate recovery intervention or deload. Complements ACWR by capturing qualitative structure, not just magnitude. |
| **Required Data** | 7 days of daily load totals (same source as Monotony and ACWR). |
| **Decision Making Impact** | High strain triggers mandatory sport scientist review of the next microcycle. Coaches may be instructed to insert a recovery day or reduce total volume. Medical staff increase vigilance for upper respiratory symptoms and sleep disturbance. |

---

### 12.8 Movement Index

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Objectify movement quality, inter-limb asymmetry, and physical capacity to identify biomechanical risk factors that predispose athletes to non-contact and overuse injuries. |
| **Variables** | Screening test results: FMS composite score (0–21), Y-Balance anterior/posteromedial/posterolateral reach distances, hop test limb symmetry index (LSI, %), drop jump reactive strength index (RSI), isokinetic quadriceps/hamstring peak torque ratio. Asymmetry flagging rules (e.g., > 10% inter-limb difference = asymmetry). Historical deficit flags from prior screenings. |
| **Calculation Logic** | Each test is scored as pass/fail or percentile against position-specific normative data. **Movement Index = Base Score − Σ Deficit Penalties**. Base Score starts at 100. Penalties applied for: each failed test (−5 to −15 depending on severity), each asymmetry > 10% (−10), each historical deficit without documented improvement (−5). Penalty magnitudes are configurable by the sport scientist based on organizational research priorities. |
| **Interpretation** | **> 85:** Low movement risk — no significant deficits or asymmetries; standard training cleared. **70–85:** Moderate risk — one or more minor deficits; targeted prehab recommended. **55–70:** High risk — significant asymmetry or failed capacity test; corrective exercise program mandatory and high-speed work may be restricted. **< 55:** Poor movement quality / elevated injury risk — comprehensive rehabilitation assessment required before full return to sport. |
| **Practical Use** | Identify athletes requiring targeted corrective exercise, manual therapy, or movement retraining. Track re-screening improvements to validate intervention effectiveness. Feed deficit flags into the Injury Risk Index. |
| **Required Data** | Periodic physical screening assessment results entered by qualified testers. Minimum one screening battery per athlete per season; recommended re-screening every 6–8 weeks or post-injury. |
| **Decision Making Impact** | Physiotherapists and strength coaches design individualized corrective programs. Coaches restrict high-intensity change-of-direction or plyometric work for athletes with significant asymmetry until re-screened. Sport scientists track population-level deficit trends to inform preseason program design. |

---

### 12.9 Injury Risk Index

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Integrate all available monitoring streams into a single composite probability score that ranks athletes by their current likelihood of suffering a fatigue-related or overuse injury within the next 7–14 days. Serve as the primary decision-support signal for daily operations. |
| **Variables** | ACWR risk zone weight (0–25); Wellness deviation penalty (0–25); Recovery Index penalty (0–20); Movement Index penalty (0–15); Sleep quality deficit (0–10); historical injury recurrence flag (0–10); contextual modifiers: age, position exposure, match density (0–5). All variables normalized to 0–100 before weighting. |
| **Calculation Logic** | **Injury Risk Index (IRI) = Σ (Normalized Variable × Variable Weight)**. Default weights: ACWR 0.25, Wellness 0.25, Recovery 0.20, Movement 0.15, Sleep 0.10, History 0.05. Weights are fully configurable and version-controlled. A **Risk Multiplier** is applied if two or more sub-indices are simultaneously in their highest risk tier (multiplicative interaction term to capture compounding risk). Final score normalized to 0–100. |
| **Interpretation** | **0–30 (Green):** Low risk — standard training, no special action. **30–50 (Yellow):** Moderate risk — standard monitoring, review modifiable drivers (sleep, recovery). **50–75 (Red):** High risk — consider load reduction, targeted medical screening, or modified participation. **75–100 (Crimson):** Critical risk — high probability of near-term injury; mandatory coach-medical-sport-science discussion before full training or competition exposure. |
| **Practical Use** | Primary daily triage tool. Coaches review the team risk grid before every session. Medical staff use the index to prioritize screening and treatment-room allocation. Sport scientists use component breakdown to investigate which modifiable factors are driving risk in flagged athletes. |
| **Required Data** | All constituent indices: ACWR, Wellness Index, Recovery Index, Movement Index, Sleep metrics, Injury History. Minimum viable IRI requires ACWR + Wellness + Load data; Movement and History improve precision but are not mandatory for initial deployment. |
| **Decision Making Impact** | Coaches make individualized session modifications (drill substitution, volume reduction). Medical staff schedule preventive screenings. Sport scientists review periodization and load distribution. The most frequently referenced metric for daily operational decisions across all staff roles. |

---

### 12.10 Readiness Index

| Attribute | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Purpose** | Provide a daily, actionable composite indicating an athlete's preparedness to undertake the prescribed training load. Designed to be the single most coach-facing metric: a clear "green light / yellow light / red light" signal for training participation. |
| **Variables** | Recovery Index (positive contributor); Wellness Index (positive contributor); inverse Fatigue Index penalty; inverse Injury Risk Index penalty; ACWR status bonus/penalty; sleep duration adequacy (binary bonus if > 7 hours or individual target). |
| **Calculation Logic** | **Readiness Index = (0.30 × Recovery Index + 0.25 × Wellness Index + 0.20 × (100 − Fatigue Index) + 0.15 × (100 − Injury Risk Index) + 0.10 × ACWR Bonus/Penalty)**. ACWR Bonus = +10 if ACWR in optimal zone (0.80–1.30); Penalty = −20 if ACWR > 1.50. Sleep bonus: +5 if duration meets individual target. Final score clamped to 0–100. |
| **Interpretation** | **> 80 (Green):** Fully ready — complete training prescription appropriate. **60–80 (Yellow):** Ready with caution — minor limitations; consider reducing high-speed or high-impact work. **40–60 (Amber/Orange):** Compromised readiness — modified session or individual program required. **< 40 (Red):** Not ready — high probability of poor adaptation or injury; alternative session (recovery, pool, bike) or rest recommended. |
| **Practical Use** | Morning athlete-facing feedback ("Your readiness today: 72 — Caution. Prioritize technique over intensity"). Coach planning grid for immediate session decision-making. The most visible and consumed metric in the FIIM ecosystem. |
| **Required Data** | Daily wellness, recovery, sleep, fatigue, and ACWR data. Minimum: wellness + load data for a functional score. Full precision requires all constituent indices. |
| **Decision Making Impact** | Direct input to daily session planning, individual drill modification, and competitive selection. Athletes self-regulate based on personal readiness feedback. Coaches communicate confidently with medical staff using a shared, interpretable metric. If an athlete is red-flagged, the coaching staff is expected to document the operational response (modification or rest) in the session log. |

### 12.11 Framework Governance & Configuration

| Governance Rule | Description |
|-------------------|-------------|
| **Algorithm Version Control** | Every index formula and weight set is versioned. Sport scientists can clone, modify, and A/B test new parameter sets against historical data before promoting to production. |
| **Transparency Requirement** | All formulas, normalization methods, threshold sources, and weighting logic are documented in-platform and exportable. No black-box indices are permitted in FIIM. |
| **Minimum Data Thresholds** | Indices display confidence flags when required data is missing or insufficient (e.g., ACWR requires 8 days; Movement Index requires one screening battery). Low-confidence scores are visually annotated, never hidden. |
| **Sport-Specific Calibration** | Normative ranges, screening test libraries, and load thresholds are configurable by sport, league, and organizational standards. Default configurations are based on published research in football, rugby, basketball, and athletics. |
| **Bias Monitoring** | Quarterly automated reports analyze index outcomes by demographic and positional groups to detect unintended algorithmic bias. Disparities trigger review by the sport science and ethics governance team. |
| **Medical Override** | Sports Doctors and Physiotherapists can place a manual clinical hold or clearance on any athlete, superseding the automated Readiness or Injury Risk Index. Overrides are logged, time-stamped, and visible to coaching staff. |

## 13. Calculation Engine

### 13.1 Engine Architecture Overview

The FIIM Calculation Engine is a modular, stateless computation layer designed to execute all sport-science indices as independent microservices or backend functions. Each module exposes a strict input contract (scalar values, time-series vectors, or configuration parameters) and returns a typed output schema (score, classification, confidence flag, and audit metadata).

**Design Principles:**

1. **Modularity:** Each index is implemented as an isolated service with no hard dependencies on other modules. Composite indices (e.g., Injury Risk Index, Readiness Index) consume the outputs of lower-order modules via a well-defined dependency graph.
2. **Determinism:** Given identical inputs and configuration, the engine must produce bit-for-bit identical outputs. No randomness, no hidden state.
3. **Idempotency:** Re-running a calculation for the same date and athlete with unchanged inputs yields the same result, enabling safe replay and reconciliation.
4. **Transparency:** Every formula, weight, threshold, and normalization parameter is stored in versioned configuration objects. Sport scientists can clone, modify, and A/B test parameter sets.
5. **Graceful Degradation:** If optional inputs (e.g., HRV, screening data) are missing, the engine redistributes weights and appends a `confidence` field to the output. Scores are never hidden due to missing data.

**Standard Normalization Helper:**

All z-score normalizations in the engine use the same deterministic helper:

```
ZSCORE(x, μ, σ) = (x - μ) / σ

NORMALIZE(x, μ, σ):
    IF σ = 0 OR x IS NULL:
        RETURN NULL
    z = ZSCORE(x, μ, σ)
    RETURN CLAMP(50 + (10 × z), 0, 100)
```

Where `μ` = individual 28-day rolling mean and `σ` = individual 28-day rolling standard deviation. The result is a 0–100 score where the athlete's 28-day average maps to 50, and each standard deviation shifts the score by 10 points.

**Standard Classification Helper:**

```
CLASSIFY(score, thresholds):
    IF score IS NULL: RETURN "insufficient_data"
    IF score ≥ thresholds.critical: RETURN "critical"
    IF score ≥ thresholds.high:      RETURN "high"
    IF score ≥ thresholds.moderate:  RETURN "moderate"
    RETURN "low"
```

### 13.2 Training Load

#### Formula

**Internal Load (sRPE):**

```
sRPE_session = Session_Duration_Minutes × Session_RPE

Daily_Internal_Load = Σ(sRPE_session) for all sessions on date D
```

**External Load (GPS / Accelerometer):**

```
Daily_External_Load_Metric = Σ(session_value) for all sessions on date D
```

**Weekly Load:**

```
Weekly_Load(date, metric) = Σ(Daily_Load(d, metric)) for d = date-6 to date
```

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Range / Unit | Source |
|----------|------|--------------|--------|
| `Session_Duration_Minutes` | Integer | 1–300 | Manual entry or wearable sync |
| `Session_RPE` | Float | 1.0–10.0 (CR-10) or 6–20 (Borg) | Athlete post-session input |
| `sRPE_session` | Float | AU | Computed |
| `Daily_Internal_Load` | Float | AU | Computed |
| `Daily_External_Load_Metric` | Float | Metric-specific (m, AU, count) | GPS/wearable API or file upload |
| `Weekly_Load` | Float | AU or metric unit | Computed |

#### Score Range

Training Load is a **raw magnitude metric**, not a normalized score. Range is unbounded and athlete-specific. The engine outputs absolute values with percentile context.

#### Interpretation

| Context | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Underload** | Weekly load < 80% of planned target OR < 0.80 ACWR |
| **Target** | Weekly load 80–100% of planned target |
| **Overload** | Weekly load > 110% of planned target OR ACWR > 1.30 |

#### Output

```json
{
  "athlete_id": "uuid",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "metric": "sRPE | total_distance | player_load | ...",
  "daily_total": 450.0,
  "weekly_total": 2850.0,
  "rolling_7d": 2850.0,
  "rolling_14d": 5400.0,
  "rolling_28d": 11200.0,
  "planned_weekly": 3000.0,
  "variance_percent": -5.0,
  "percentile_in_squad": 62.5,
  "session_breakdown": [...]
}
```

#### Example

| Session | Duration | RPE | sRPE |
|---------|----------|-----|------|
| Morning technical | 90 min | 5 | 450 |
| Afternoon strength | 45 min | 7 | 315 |

```
Daily_Internal_Load = 450 + 315 = 765 AU

```

### 13.3 ACWR (Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio)

#### Formula

**Rolling Average Method:**

```
Acute_Load(date)   = Σ(Daily_Load(d)) for d = date-6 to date
Chronic_Load(date) = Σ(Daily_Load(d)) for d = date-27 to date
Chronic_Mean       = Chronic_Load / 4

ACWR_Rolling(date) = Acute_Load / Chronic_Mean
```

**EWMA Method:**

```
λ_acute   = 2 / (N_acute + 1)     // default N_acute = 7  → λ = 0.25
λ_chronic = 2 / (N_chronic + 1)   // default N_chronic = 28 → λ = 0.069

EWMA_acute(t)   = λ_acute   × Load(t) + (1 - λ_acute)   × EWMA_acute(t-1)
EWMA_chronic(t) = λ_chronic × Load(t) + (1 - λ_chronic) × EWMA_chronic(t-1)

ACWR_EWMA(t) = EWMA_acute(t) / EWMA_chronic(t)
```

**Note:** For the first valid calculation, the engine requires `max(N_acute, N_chronic)` + 1 historical days. Before this, the output is `NULL` with confidence `"insufficient_data"`.

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Default | Description |
|----------|------|---------|-------------|
| `Load(t)` | Float | AU | Daily total for selected metric |
| `N_acute` | Integer | 7 | Days in acute window |
| `N_chronic` | Integer | 28 | Days in chronic window |
| `method` | String | "rolling" | "rolling" or "ewma" |
| `exclude_non_training_days` | Boolean | false | If true, rest days (load = 0) are excluded from chronic baseline |

#### Score Range

ACWR is a **unitless ratio**. Theoretically unbounded above zero. Typical observed range: 0.30–2.50.

#### Interpretation

| Zone | ACWR Range | Classification | Action |
|------|------------|----------------|--------|
| Underload | < 0.80 | low | Detraining risk; consider increasing load |
| Optimal | 0.80 – 1.30 | optimal | Safe adaptation zone |
| High Risk | 1.30 – 1.50 | high | Elevated injury risk; reduce or modify |
| Very High | > 1.50 | critical | Acute spike; high probability of tissue failure |

#### Output

```json
{
  "athlete_id": "uuid",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "metric": "sRPE",
  "method": "rolling",
  "acute_load": 3150.0,
  "chronic_mean": 2800.0,
  "acwr": 1.125,
  "zone": "optimal",
  "confidence": "full",
  "days_of_history": 45
}
```

#### Example

**Rolling Average:**

```
Acute (last 7 days):  420 + 380 + 450 + 500 + 300 + 550 + 550 = 3,150
Chronic (last 28 days): 12,600 total → Chronic_Mean = 12,600 / 4 = 3,150
ACWR = 3,150 / 3,150 = 1.00  → Optimal
```

**EWMA (same daily loads, today = 550):**

```
λ_acute = 0.25, λ_chronic = 0.069
EWMA_acute(today)  ≈ 520 (smoothed recent trend)
EWMA_chronic(today) ≈ 3,100 (long-term stable trend)
ACWR_EWMA = 520 / 3,100 ≈ 1.68  → Critical
```

*Note:* The EWMA method is more sensitive to recent spikes and may flag risk earlier than rolling average.

### 13.4 Monotony

#### Formula

```
Microcycle_Loads = [Load(D-6), Load(D-5), ..., Load(D)]

Mean_Load = MEAN(Microcycle_Loads)
Std_Load  = STDEV(Microcycle_Loads)

IF Std_Load = 0:
    IF Mean_Load = 0:
        RETURN {"monotony": null, "note": "complete_rest_week"}
    ELSE:
        RETURN {"monotony": null, "note": "maximum_monotony"}

Monotony = Mean_Load / Std_Load
```

**Non-Training Day Handling (configurable):**

```
IF exclude_rest_days = true:
    Microcycle_Loads = FILTER(Microcycle_Loads, value > 0)
```

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Description |
|----------|------|-------------|
| `Microcycle_Loads` | Array[Float] | 7 daily load values ending on calculation date |
| `Mean_Load` | Float | Arithmetic mean of the 7 values |
| `Std_Load` | Float | Sample standard deviation (N-1 denominator) |
| `exclude_rest_days` | Boolean | If true, zero-load days are excluded from the array |

#### Score Range

Monotony is a **unitless ratio**. Theoretically ≥ 0. Observed range: 1.0–10.0+. Standard deviation of 0 returns `NULL` with an explanatory flag.

#### Interpretation

| Range | Classification | Meaning |
|-------|--------------|---------|
| < 2.0 | low | Good variability; recovery days present |
| 2.0 – 2.5 | moderate | Acceptable for some phases; monitor wellness |
| > 2.5 | high | Repetitive pattern; overreaching/illness risk |

#### Output

```json
{
  "athlete_id": "uuid",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "metric": "sRPE",
  "monotony": 2.8,
  "mean_load": 450.0,
  "std_load": 160.7,
  "classification": "high",
  "exclude_rest_days": false,
  "confidence": "full"
}
```

#### Example

```
Week loads (sRPE): [500, 480, 520, 0, 550, 530, 510]
Mean = 3,090 / 7 = 441.4
Std  = sqrt(Σ(xi - 441.4)² / 6) = 184.3
Monotony = 441.4 / 184.3 = 2.39  → Moderate

If rest day excluded: [500, 480, 520, 550, 530, 510]
Mean = 516.7, Std = 24.8, Monotony = 20.8  → Maximum monotony flag
```

### 13.5 Strain

#### Formula

```
Weekly_Load = Σ(Load(d)) for d = D-6 to D

IF Monotony IS NULL:
    Strain = NULL
    Confidence = "insufficient_data"
ELSE:
    Strain = Weekly_Load × Monotony
```

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Source |
|----------|------|--------|
| `Weekly_Load` | Float | Sum of 7 daily loads |
| `Monotony` | Float | Output from Monotony module |

#### Score Range

Strain is expressed in **arbitrary units (AU)**. Range is athlete- and sport-specific. Typical sRPE-based values: 2,000–10,000 AU.

#### Interpretation

| Threshold (sRPE-based) | Classification | Action |
|------------------------|--------------|--------|
| < 3,000 | low | Standard microcycle stress |
| 3,000 – 4,000 | moderate | Monitor recovery markers |
| 4,000 – 5,000 | high | Insert recovery day or deload |
| > 5,000 | critical | High overreaching risk; mandatory review |

*Note:* Thresholds must be calibrated by sport scientist for the specific sport and load metric.

#### Output

```json
{
  "athlete_id": "uuid",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "metric": "sRPE",
  "weekly_load": 3500.0,
  "monotony": 2.8,
  "strain": 9800.0,
  "classification": "critical",
  "threshold_used": 5000.0,
  "confidence": "full"
}
```

#### Example

```
Weekly_Load = 3,500 AU
Monotony    = 2.8
Strain      = 3,500 × 2.8 = 9,800 AU  → Critical
```

### 13.6 Fatigue Index

#### Formula

```
Step 1 — Normalize each variable using the athlete's 28-day μ and σ:

N_Fatigue    = NORMALIZE(Fatigue_Rating, μ_fatigue, σ_fatigue)
N_Soreness   = NORMALIZE(Soreness_Rating, μ_soreness, σ_soreness)
N_SleepDef   = NORMALIZE(Sleep_Deficit_Score, μ_sleepdef, σ_sleepdef)
N_LoadPct    = 100 × PERCENTILE(7Day_Load, 28Day_Load_History)

IF HRV_RMSSD IS NOT NULL:
    N_HRV    = NORMALIZE(HRV_RMSSD, μ_hrv, σ_hrv)
    HRV_Penalty = 100 - N_HRV   // lower HRV = higher penalty
ELSE:
    HRV_Penalty = NULL

Step 2 — Redistribute weights if HRV is missing:

Base_Weights = [0.30, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10]
Active_Indices = [N_Fatigue, N_Soreness, N_SleepDef, N_LoadPct, HRV_Penalty]
Active_Weights = REDISTRIBUTE(Base_Weights, Active_Indices)

Step 3 — Compute weighted sum:

Fatigue_Index = Σ(Active_Indices[i] × Active_Weights[i])
```

**Sleep Deficit Score:**

```
Target_Sleep = athlete.config.target_sleep_hours  // default 8.0
Actual_Sleep = sleep_duration_hours
Sleep_Deficit_Score = MAX(0, (Target_Sleep - Actual_Sleep) / Target_Sleep × 100)
```

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Range | Source |
|----------|------|-------|--------|
| `Fatigue_Rating` | Integer | 1–10 | Daily wellness survey |
| `Soreness_Rating` | Integer | 1–10 | Daily wellness survey |
| `Sleep_Deficit_Score` | Float | 0–100 | Computed from sleep duration vs. target |
| `7Day_Load` | Float | AU | Training Load module |
| `HRV_RMSSD` | Float | ms | Wearable sync (optional) |
| `μ_28, σ_28` | Float | — | Rolling 28-day individual statistics |

#### Score Range

0–100. Higher score = greater fatigue.

#### Interpretation

| Score | Zone | Action |
|-------|------|--------|
| 0–30 | low | Full capacity; no modification |
| 30–50 | moderate | Standard monitoring |
| 50–75 | high | Reduce volume or intensity; medical check-in if sustained |
| 75–100 | critical | Mandatory sport science / medical review; likely overreaching |

#### Output

```json
{
  "athlete_id": "uuid",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "fatigue_index": 64.2,
  "classification": "high",
  "components": {
    "normalized_fatigue":   {"value": 72.0, "weight": 0.30},
    "normalized_soreness":  {"value": 68.0, "weight": 0.25},
    "sleep_deficit":        {"value": 55.0, "weight": 0.20},
    "load_percentile":      {"value": 62.0, "weight": 0.15},
    "hrv_penalty":          {"value": null, "weight": 0.00}
  },
  "confidence": "partial_hrv_missing",
  "z_score_basis": "28_day_individual"
}
```

#### Example

```
Inputs:
  Fatigue = 7 (μ=4.5, σ=1.2)  → N_Fatigue = 50 + 10×(2.08) = 70.8
  Soreness = 6 (μ=3.0, σ=1.5)  → N_Soreness = 50 + 10×(2.0) = 70.0
  Sleep deficit = 25% (μ=10%, σ=8%) → N_SleepDef = 50 + 10×(1.875) = 68.8
  7-day load percentile = 70th percentile → N_LoadPct = 70.0
  HRV = null (missing)

Weights redistribute from [0.30, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10] to [0.333, 0.278, 0.222, 0.167, 0.00]

Fatigue_Index = (70.8×0.333) + (70.0×0.278) + (68.8×0.222) + (70.0×0.167)
              = 23.6 + 19.5 + 15.3 + 11.7
              = 70.1  → High
```

### 13.7 Recovery Index

#### Formula

```
// Sleep Duration Score
Sleep_Duration_Score = MIN(100, (Actual_Sleep_Hours / Target_Sleep_Hours) × 100)

// Sleep Quality Score (direct mapping)
Sleep_Quality_Score = Sleep_Quality_Rating × 20   // 1→20, 5→100

Sleep_Score = (0.60 × Sleep_Duration_Score) + (0.40 × Sleep_Quality_Score)

// HRV Score (optional)
IF HRV_RMSSD IS NOT NULL:
    HRV_Z = ZSCORE(HRV_RMSSD, μ_hrv, σ_hrv)
    HRV_Score = MAX(0, MIN(100, 100 - (ABS(HRV_Z) × 20)))
ELSE:
    HRV_Score = NULL

// Subjective Recovery Score
Recovery_Rating_Score = Recovery_Rating × 10   // 1→10, 10→100

// Rest Day Bonus
Rest_Day_Bonus = IF(today_load = 0, 10, 0)

// Weighted assembly
Base_Weights = [0.40, 0.30, 0.20, 0.10]
Active_Indices = [Sleep_Score, HRV_Score, Recovery_Rating_Score, Rest_Day_Bonus]
Active_Weights = REDISTRIBUTE(Base_Weights, Active_Indices)

Recovery_Index = Σ(Active_Indices[i] × Active_Weights[i])
```

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Range | Source |
|----------|------|-------|--------|
| `Actual_Sleep_Hours` | Float | 0–12 | Daily sleep survey or wearable |
| `Target_Sleep_Hours` | Float | 6–10 | Athlete config (default 8.0) |
| `Sleep_Quality_Rating` | Integer | 1–5 | Daily wellness survey |
| `HRV_RMSSD` | Float | ms | Wearable sync (optional) |
| `Recovery_Rating` | Integer | 1–10 | Daily wellness survey (optional) |
| `today_load` | Float | AU | Training Load module |

#### Score Range

0–100. Higher score = better recovery.

#### Interpretation

| Score | Zone | Training Prescription |
|-------|------|----------------------|
| > 85 | excellent | Cleared for high-intensity and high-speed work |
| 70–85 | adequate | Standard training prescription |
| 55–70 | incomplete | Reduce high-speed and power work |
| < 55 | poor | Withhold high-intensity work; prioritize sleep/recovery |

#### Output

```json
{
  "athlete_id": "uuid",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "recovery_index": 78.4,
  "classification": "adequate",
  "components": {
    "sleep_score": {"value": 82.0, "weight": 0.40, "duration": 7.5, "quality": 4},
    "hrv_score": {"value": 75.0, "weight": 0.30, "rmssd": 42},
    "subjective_recovery": {"value": 70.0, "weight": 0.20, "rating": 7},
    "rest_day_bonus": {"value": 0, "weight": 0.10}
  },
  "confidence": "full"
}
```

#### Example

```
Actual Sleep = 7.5 hours, Target = 8.0
Duration Score = MIN(100, 7.5/8.0 × 100) = 93.8
Quality = 4 → Quality Score = 80
Sleep Score = (0.60 × 93.8) + (0.40 × 80) = 56.3 + 32.0 = 88.3

HRV: RMSSD = 42, μ=45, σ=8
HRV_Z = (42-45)/8 = -0.375
HRV_Score = 100 - (0.375 × 20) = 92.5

Recovery Rating = 7 → 70
Rest Day Bonus = 0 (load > 0)

Weights: [0.40, 0.30, 0.20, 0.10] (all present)
Recovery_Index = (88.3×0.40) + (92.5×0.30) + (70×0.20) + (0×0.10)
               = 35.3 + 27.8 + 14.0 + 0
               = 77.1  → Adequate
```

### 13.8 Workload Index

#### Formula

```
Daily_Workload = Daily_Internal_Load + Σ(External_Metrics_Normalized)

// External metrics are normalized to internal sRPE scale via sport-scientist-defined
// conversion factors (e.g., 100 m HSR = 2.5 AU). If no mapping exists, they are tracked
// separately and the Workload Index uses internal load only.

Weekly_Workload(date) = Σ(Daily_Workload(d)) for d = date-6 to date

Rolling_Average(date, window) = Σ(Daily_Workload(d)) / window
                                for d = date-(window-1) to date

Planned_vs_Actual_Variance = ((Weekly_Workload - Planned_Weekly) / Planned_Weekly) × 100

Percentile_Rank = (number of squad members with lower weekly workload / total squad members) × 100
```

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Description |
|----------|------|-------------|
| `Daily_Internal_Load` | Float | sRPE total |
| `External_Metrics` | Object | Key-value pairs per metric (distance, player_load, etc.) |
| `External_Conversion` | Object | Mapping factors to normalize external metrics onto sRPE scale |
| `Planned_Weekly` | Float | Target set by sport scientist in periodization module |
| `window` | Integer | Rolling window in days (7, 14, 21, 28) |

#### Score Range

Raw magnitude; unbounded. The engine outputs absolute values plus percentile and variance percentages.

#### Interpretation

| Variance | Status |
|----------|--------|
| < -20% | Significantly underloaded |
| -20% to -10% | Mildly underloaded |
| -10% to +10% | On target |
| +10% to +20% | Mildly overloaded |
| > +20% | Significantly overloaded |

#### Output

```json
{
  "athlete_id": "uuid",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "daily_workload": 765.0,
  "weekly_workload": 4225.0,
  "rolling_7d": 4225.0,
  "rolling_14d": 8100.0,
  "rolling_28d": 16800.0,
  "planned_weekly": 4000.0,
  "variance_percent": 5.6,
  "variance_status": "on_target",
  "percentile_in_squad": 58.0,
  "external_metrics": {
    "total_distance": 6500,

    "player_load": 420
  }
}
```

#### Example

```
Daily:
  sRPE = 765 AU
  Total distance = 6,500 m (no conversion applied in this org)

  sRPE sum = 4,225 AU
  Planned = 4,000 AU
```

### 13.9 Movement Index

#### Formula

```
Base_Score = 100

FOR each test in Screening_Battery:
    IF test.result < test.normative_threshold:
        Severity = test.severity_weight  // 5, 10, or 15
        Base_Score -= Severity
        
    IF test.asymmetry_percent > 10 AND test.asymmetry_side IS NOT NULL:
        Base_Score -= 10

FOR each historical_deficit in unresolved_deficits:
    IF deficit.status = "open" AND days_since_screening > 42:
        Base_Score -= 5

Movement_Index = MAX(0, Base_Score)
```

**Test Severity Weights (default, configurable):**

| Deficit Type | Default Penalty |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Minor (e.g., FMS 1) | -5 |
| Moderate (e.g., FMS 0, Y-Balance < 95%) | -10 |
| Major (e.g., hop LSI < 85%, isokinetic Q/H < 50%) | -15 |

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Description |
|----------|------|-------------|
| `Screening_Battery` | Array | List of tests with results, thresholds, and side data |
| `test.result` | Float | Raw test score |
| `test.normative_threshold` | Float | Pass/fail cutoff (position-specific) |
| `test.asymmetry_percent` | Float | % difference between left and right |
| `unresolved_deficits` | Array | Historical deficits flagged in prior screenings without documented improvement |

#### Score Range

0–100. Higher score = lower movement risk.

#### Interpretation

| Score | Zone | Action |
|-------|------|--------|
| > 85 | low | Standard training; routine prehab |
| 70–85 | moderate | Targeted prehab for flagged deficits |
| 55–70 | high | Mandatory corrective program; restrict high-speed COD/plyos |
| < 55 | critical | Comprehensive rehab assessment required |

#### Output

```json
{
  "athlete_id": "uuid",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "screening_date": "2026-06-15",
  "movement_index": 75,
  "classification": "moderate",
  "penalties": [
    {"test": "FMS_Hurdle_Step", "severity": 5, "reason": "score_below_threshold"},
    {"test": "Y_Balance_ANT", "severity": 10, "reason": "asymmetry_12pct"}
  ],
  "confidence": "full"
}
```

#### Example

```
Base = 100
FMS Deep Squat = 2 (threshold 2) → pass, no penalty
FMS Hurdle Step = 1 (threshold 2) → minor deficit → -5  → 95
Y-Balance Anterior Left = 95 cm, Right = 107 cm
  Asymmetry = (107-95)/107 = 11.2% > 10% → -10  → 85
Hop Test LSI = 92% (threshold 90%) → pass, no penalty

  → -5  → 80
```

### 13.10 Injury Risk Index

#### Formula

```
Step 1 — Normalize and invert constituent inputs to 0–100 risk scale:

Risk_ACWR      = ACWR_Risk_Score    // 0 (optimal) to 100 (critical)
Risk_Wellness  = 100 - Wellness_Index
Risk_Recovery  = 100 - Recovery_Index
Risk_Movement  = 100 - Movement_Index
Risk_Sleep     = Sleep_Deficit_Score   // 0–100
Risk_History   = IF(prior_fatigue_injury_within_12mo, 100, 0)

Step 2 — Apply default weights (configurable):

Weights = [0.25, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10, 0.05]
Indices = [Risk_ACWR, Risk_Wellness, Risk_Recovery, Risk_Movement, Risk_Sleep, Risk_History]
Active_Weights = REDISTRIBUTE(Weights, Indices)

Base_Risk = Σ(Indices[i] × Active_Weights[i])

Step 3 — Interaction multiplier (compounding risk):

High_Risk_Count = COUNT(Indices[i] > 75)
IF High_Risk_Count ≥ 2:
    Multiplier = 1.0 + (0.10 × High_Risk_Count)
    Final_Risk = MIN(100, Base_Risk × Multiplier)
ELSE:
    Final_Risk = Base_Risk
```

**ACWR Risk Score Mapping:**

```
ACWR_Risk_Score =
    IF acwr < 0.80:   20
    IF acwr ≤ 1.30:   MAX(0, (acwr - 0.80) / 0.50 × 20)  // 0–20
    IF acwr ≤ 1.50:   20 + ((acwr - 1.30) / 0.20 × 40)   // 20–60
    IF acwr > 1.50:    60 + MIN(40, (acwr - 1.50) × 40)   // 60–100
```

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Source |
|----------|------|--------|
| `acwr` | Float | ACWR module |
| `wellness_index` | Float | Wellness Index module |
| `recovery_index` | Float | Recovery Index module |
| `movement_index` | Float | Movement Index module |
| `sleep_deficit_score` | Float | Sleep module (0–100) |
| `prior_fatigue_injury_within_12mo` | Boolean | Injury Management module |
| `weights` | Array[Float] | Sport-scientist configuration (versioned) |

#### Interpretation

| Score | Color | Zone | Action |
|-------|-------|------|--------|
| 0–30 | Green | low | Standard training |
| 30–50 | Yellow | moderate | Monitor; review modifiable drivers |
| 50–75 | Red | high | Load reduction or modified participation; medical screening |
| 75–100 | Crimson | critical | Mandatory staff discussion before full exposure; likely near-term injury |

```json
    {"name": "history", "risk_score": 100, "weight": 0.05}

```

#### Example

```
Inputs:
  ACWR = 1.45  → ACWR_Risk_Score = 20 + ((1.45-1.30)/0.20 × 40) = 50
  Wellness_Index = 55  → Risk_Wellness = 45
  Recovery_Index = 40  → Risk_Recovery = 60
  Movement_Index = 30  → Risk_Movement = 70
  Sleep_Deficit = 50%  → Risk_Sleep = 50

All present, weights unchanged:
Base_Risk = (50×0.25) + (45×0.25) + (60×0.20) + (70×0.15) + (50×0.10) + (100×0.05)
          = 12.5 + 11.25 + 12.0 + 10.5 + 5.0 + 5.0
          = 56.25

High_Risk_Count = COUNT([50,45,60,70,50,100] > 75) = 2  (movement and history)
Multiplier = 1.0 + (0.10 × 2) = 1.20

Final_Risk = MIN(100, 56.25 × 1.20) = 67.5  → High (upper bound)
```

### 13.11 Readiness Index

#### Formula

```
Step 1 — Assemble inputs:

RI = Recovery_Index
WI = Wellness_Index
FI = Fatigue_Index
IRI = Injury_Risk_Index
ACWR = acwr_ratio

Step 2 — Compute inverse penalties and bonuses:

Inverse_Fatigue    = 100 - FI
Inverse_Risk       = 100 - IRI

IF ACWR ≥ 0.80 AND ACWR ≤ 1.30:
    ACWR_Bonus = +10
ELIF ACWR > 1.50:
    ACWR_Penalty = -20
ELSE:
    ACWR_Bonus = 0

Sleep_Bonus = IF(Actual_Sleep ≥ Target_Sleep, +5, 0)

Step 3 — Weighted assembly:

Readiness_Index = (0.30 × RI) + (0.25 × WI) + (0.20 × Inverse_Fatigue)
                + (0.15 × Inverse_Risk) + (0.10 × ACWR_Bonus)
                + Sleep_Bonus

Readiness_Index = CLAMP(Readiness_Index, 0, 100)
```

**Note:** The ACWR and Sleep bonuses are additive modifiers applied after the weighted sum. If ACWR penalty applies, it is additive (−20).

#### Variables

| Variable | Type | Source |
|----------|------|--------|
| `Recovery_Index` | Float | Recovery Index module |
| `Wellness_Index` | Float | Wellness Index module |
| `Fatigue_Index` | Float | Fatigue Index module |
| `Injury_Risk_Index` | Float | Injury Risk Index module |
| `acwr_ratio` | Float | ACWR module |
| `Actual_Sleep` | Float | Sleep survey/wearable |
| `Target_Sleep` | Float | Athlete config |

#### Score Range

0–100. Higher score = greater readiness for training.

#### Interpretation

| Score | Color | Zone | Prescription |
|-------|-------|------|--------------|
| > 80 | Green | fully_ready | Complete training prescription |
| 60–80 | Yellow | ready_with_caution | Minor limits; reduce high-speed/high-impact |
| 40–60 | Amber | compromised | Modified session or individual program |
| < 40 | Red | not_ready | Alternative session or rest; medical review if sustained |

```json
    "input_checksum": "sha256:..."
  }

```

**Dependency Graph:**

```
Training Load
    ↓
ACWR ←──────┐
Monotony ←──┤
Strain ←────┤
            ↓
Fatigue Index ← Wellness Index ← Daily Wellness
                ← Recovery Index ← Sleep / HRV
                ← Workload Index

Movement Index ← Physical Screening

Injury Risk Index ← ACWR Risk Score
                  ← Wellness Inverse
                  ← Recovery Inverse
                  ← Movement Inverse
                  ← Sleep Deficit
                  ← Injury History

Readiness Index ← Recovery
                ← Wellness
                ← Inverse Fatigue
                ← Inverse Injury Risk
                ← ACWR Bonus/Penalty
                ← Sleep Bonus
```

**Execution Order:**

1. Training Load (daily batch or real-time on upload)
2. ACWR, Monotony, Strain (triggered after daily load finalization)
3. Wellness Index, Recovery Index, Fatigue Index, Workload Index (triggered after morning wellness window closes)
4. Movement Index (triggered on screening data entry)
5. Injury Risk Index (triggered after all sub-indices are available; recalculates if any sub-index changes)
6. Readiness Index (triggered last; consumed by dashboards and athlete mobile view)

## 14. Dashboard Design

### 14.1 Dashboard Architecture Overview

FIIM dashboards are role-specific data surfaces that transform raw monitoring outputs into actionable intelligence. Each dashboard is composed of modular widgets (Cards, Charts, Tables, Alert Streams) that consume filtered, scoped data from the calculation engine and backend modules.

**Design Principles:**

1. **Role-Based Data Scoping:** Every widget respects RBAC. An athlete cannot see team aggregates; a coach cannot see medical notes.
2. **Time-Aware Defaults:** All dashboards default to "today" but support configurable date ranges with persistent user preferences.
3. **Progressive Disclosure:** Summary-level data is visible at first glance; drill-down reveals individual profiles, raw inputs, and historical trends.
4. **Alert-Driven Prioritization:** High-risk or unacknowledged items are surfaced at the top of every dashboard, regardless of role.
5. **Mobile-First Responsiveness:** Coach and medical dashboards must be fully functional on tablets; athlete dashboard is mobile-native.

## 15. Database Requirements

### 15.1 Data Model Overview

The FIIM database is designed as a relational core with time-series optimized storage for high-frequency monitoring data. The model enforces strict referential integrity for identity, governance, and medical data while allowing flexible schema evolution for sport-science calculations and screening batteries.

**Design Principles:**

1. **Temporal Accuracy:** All monitoring data is tied to effective dates. Historical records are immutable; updates create new versions with validity periods.
2. **Medical Data Segregation:** Clinical entities (injury cases, treatment notes, diagnoses) are logically isolated and protected by RBAC at the database access layer.
3. **Audit Immutability:** Audit logs and calculation histories are append-only and non-deletable by application users.
4. **Multi-Tenancy:** All entities scoped to an `organization_id` to support SaaS deployment with strict tenant isolation.
5. **Configurability:** Screening tests, wellness dimensions, and algorithm parameters are data-driven, not hard-coded.

## 17. MVP Scope

The Minimum Viable Product (MVP) of FIIM delivers the core value proposition—**proactive injury prevention through evidence-based monitoring**—to a single primary market segment (professional sports clubs and high-performance academies) with a focused feature set that can be launched, validated, and iterated upon within a constrained development timeline.

### 17.1 In-Scope (MVP)

| Category | Features |
|----------|----------|
| **Authentication & RBAC** | Email/password login, SSO (SAML 2.0), role assignment (Admin, Coach, Sport Scientist, Physiotherapist, Sports Doctor, Athlete), team/squad scoping. |
| **Athlete Management** | Athlete profile creation, team/squad assignment, consent management, bulk CSV import. |
| **Daily Wellness Monitoring** | Configurable daily wellness survey (fatigue, sleep, soreness, mood, stress), mobile-optimized input, real-time wellness score calculation. |
| **Training Load Monitoring** | Manual session entry (duration, RPE), automatic sRPE calculation, daily and weekly load summaries, basic GPS/wearable file upload (CSV). |
| **ACWR Monitoring** | Rolling average ACWR calculation (7-day acute / 28-day chronic), traffic-light risk zones, team distribution view. |
| **Injury Risk Index** | Composite risk score integrating ACWR, wellness, and load data; traffic-light classification; daily team risk grid. |
| **Readiness Index** | Daily readiness score for coaches and athletes; green/yellow/amber/red classification with guidance text. |
| **Injury Management** | Injury case creation (site, tissue, mechanism), basic treatment notes, return-to-play stage tracking (5-stage model), medical hold / clearance workflow. |
| **Dashboards** | Coach Dashboard (team readiness grid, availability, alerts), Athlete Dashboard (personal readiness, wellness input, recovery feedback), Sport Scientist Dashboard (compliance, raw data explorer, threshold configuration). |
| **Alerts** | Real-time alerts for high ACWR, poor wellness, and elevated injury risk; acknowledgment workflow; push notification to mobile. |
| **Reporting** | Weekly monitoring report template (PDF export), basic filtering by team and date range. |
| **Data Export** | Raw data CSV export for sport scientists. |

### 17.2 Out-of-Scope (Post-MVP)

| Feature | Rationale for Deferral |
|---------|------------------------|
| **Wearable API Integrations** | Requires vendor partnership agreements and OAuth infrastructure; CSV upload satisfies MVP data ingestion. |
| **GPS Device Auto-Sync** | Complex mapping configurations; manual upload validates core workflow first. |
| **Monotony & Strain** | Valuable but secondary to ACWR for initial injury prevention narrative; adds complexity to periodization views. |
| **Movement Index / Physical Screening** | Requires normative data setup, tester training, and asymmetry calculation; high value but longer implementation. |
| **Recovery Index with HRV** | Depends on wearable integrations; sleep-based recovery is MVP proxy. |
| **Executive Dashboard** | Requires aggregated multi-team views and cost-of-injury analytics; single-team focus for MVP. |
| **Advanced Reporting** | Scheduled reports, custom report builder, PowerPoint export, white-label branding. |
| **In-Platform Messaging** | Email and external communication tools suffice for MVP; messaging adds moderation and compliance complexity. |
| **Machine Learning / Predictive Modeling** | Requires large historical datasets; rule-based indices deliver immediate value and transparency. |
| **Multi-Language Support** | English-only for MVP; international expansion follows product-market fit validation. |
| **Parent / Guardian Portal** | Academy-specific feature; professional clubs are the MVP beachhead. |

### 17.3 MVP Success Criteria

- Onboard 3–5 pilot customers (professional clubs or academies) within 90 days of launch.
- Achieve > 80% daily wellness compliance and > 85% session RPE completion across pilot customers.
- Demonstrate that athletes with elevated Injury Risk Index (> 75) receive preventive intervention in > 60% of cases.
- Zero critical security vulnerabilities or data breaches.
- Coach NPS > 30 among pilot users.

## 18. Release Plan

### 18.1 Release Philosophy

FIIM follows a **continuous delivery** model with quarterly named releases and bi-weekly maintenance updates. All releases are zero-downtime deployments using blue-green or rolling strategies. Feature flags decouple deployment from activation, enabling gradual rollouts and instant rollback.

### 18.2 Release Naming Convention

Releases are named by year and quarter (e.g., **FIIM 2026 Q3**, **FIIM 2026 Q4**) with incremental patch versions for maintenance (e.g., **2026 Q3.1**, **2026 Q3.2**).

### 18.3 Release Schedule

| Release | Target Date | Theme | Key Deliverables |
|---------|-------------|-------|------------------|
| **Alpha** | Month 3 | Foundation | Core auth, athlete management, basic wellness survey, manual load entry, ACWR calculation, raw data export. Internal testing only. |
| **Beta** | Month 5 | Validation | Coach dashboard, athlete dashboard, alert center, injury case management, 2 pilot customers onboarded. |
| **MVP (2026 Q3)** | Month 6 | Launch | Injury Risk Index, Readiness Index, weekly PDF reports, mobile-optimized athlete view, 3–5 paying pilot customers. |
| **2026 Q4** | Month 9 | Integration | First wearable API integration (Whoop or Garmin), GPS file auto-parse, Monotony & Strain modules, executive dashboard beta. |
| **2027 Q1** | Month 12 | Scale | Full wearable API suite, Movement Index / physical screening, scheduled reporting, parent portal (academy), self-service onboarding. |
| **2027 Q2** | Month 15 | Intelligence | Historical trend analytics, basic ML-assisted risk refinement (A/B tested), league compliance reporting templates, API v1 public release. |
| **2027 Q3** | Month 18 | Enterprise | SSO advanced (SCIM provisioning), multi-organization admin console, custom algorithm marketplace, SOC 2 Type II certification. |

### 18.4 Deployment Environment Strategy

| Environment | Purpose | Data | Access |
|-------------|---------|------|--------|
| **Development** | Feature development, unit testing | Synthetic / anonymized | Engineering only |
| **Staging** | Integration testing, UAT, demo | Anonymized clone of production (monthly refresh) | Engineering + Product + Pilot customers (UAT) |
| **Production** | Live customer data | Real customer data | End users |
| **Disaster Recovery** | Failover and restore validation | Replicated production data | Infrastructure team only |

### 18.5 Hotfix Policy

- **Critical (P1):** Security breach, data loss, complete platform outage → Fix within 4 hours, deploy within 8 hours.
- **High (P2):** Major feature broken, incorrect risk calculations, medical data visibility bug → Fix within 24 hours, deploy within 48 hours.
- **Medium (P3):** UI defects, non-critical calculation edge cases, performance degradation → Fix within 1 week, deploy with next scheduled patch.
- **Low (P4):** Cosmetic issues, documentation errors → Fix within 2 weeks, deploy with next scheduled release.

## 26. Technical Architecture

### 26.1 System Architecture Overview

FIIM is designed as a **cloud-native, multi-tenant SaaS platform** built on a **modular monolith-first** architecture that can gracefully decompose into microservices as scale and team size demand. The architecture prioritizes operational simplicity for the 6–8 engineer founding team while ensuring tenant isolation, horizontal scalability, and compliance-grade security from Day 1.

**Architectural Principles:**

1. **Modular Monolith (MVP):** Core business domains (Auth, Athlete, Wellness, Load, Injury, Calculation, Reporting) are packaged as independent modules within a single deployable unit. Boundaries are enforced via internal APIs and strict module contracts, enabling future extraction into standalone services.
2. **API-First Design:** All client functionality is exposed through a unified REST API. The web frontend, mobile PWA, and future native apps consume the same API surface.
3. **Event-Driven Async Processing:** High-latency operations (report generation, calculation batching, data imports, alert dispatch) are decoupled via a message queue.
4. **Database-per-Tenant Logical Isolation:** A single PostgreSQL cluster with row-level tenant isolation (`organization_id` on every table). Physical tenant separation (separate databases) is available as an enterprise tier post-MVP.
5. **Immutable Audit & Time-Series Streams:** Audit logs and high-frequency wearable data are streamed to append-only storage (object storage / Kafka) separate from transactional OLTP.

### 26.3 Application Architecture

#### 26.3.1 Modular Monolith Structure (MVP)

The backend is organized as a **modular monolith** with strict domain boundaries. Each module encapsulates its own controllers, services, repositories, DTOs, and domain events.

```
src/
├── modules/
│   ├── auth/                 # Authentication, SSO, MFA, session management
│   ├── rbac/                 # Roles, permissions, user-role assignments
│   ├── organization/         # Tenant management, billing, settings
│   ├── athlete/              # Athlete profiles, assignments, consent
│   ├── wellness/             # Surveys, responses, wellness index calculation
│   ├── load/                 # Training sessions, session load records, daily summaries
│   ├── screening/            # Batteries, tests, assessments, results, movement index
│   ├── injury/               # Cases, diagnoses, treatment notes, RTP, clearance
│   ├── calculation/          # ACWR, fatigue, recovery, risk, readiness indices
│   ├── alert/                # Alert generation, dispatch, acknowledgment
│   ├── reporting/            # Templates, report generation, scheduling, export
│   ├── messaging/            # In-app messages, notifications
│   └── admin/                # Admin console, user provisioning, audit queries
├── shared/
│   ├── database/             # TypeORM / Prisma configuration, migrations
│   ├── events/               # Domain event bus, event handlers
│   ├── queue/                # Message queue abstraction
│   ├── cache/                # Redis abstraction
│   ├── security/             # Encryption, hashing, JWT utilities
│   └── exceptions/           # Global exception filters
├── infrastructure/
│   ├── logger/               # Structured logging setup
│   ├── metrics/              # Prometheus metrics registry
│   └── storage/              # S3/file storage abstraction
└── main.ts                   # Application bootstrap
```

**Module Contracts:**
- Modules communicate via **domain events** (e.g., `WellnessSubmittedEvent`, `LoadUpdatedEvent`) published to an internal event bus.
- Direct service-to-service imports across module boundaries are forbidden; only the event bus and shared kernel are accessible.
- Each module exposes a **public API surface** (controllers + DTOs) and hides internal repositories and services.

#### 26.3.2 Future Service Decomposition

When a module requires independent scaling or team ownership, it extracts into a microservice with its own database and API:

| Phase | Candidate for Extraction | Trigger |
|-------|-------------------------|---------|
| Post-MVP | **Calculation Engine** | CPU-intensive batch jobs need independent scaling; dedicated data science team |
| 6–12 months | **Integration Hub** (wearables/GPS) | High volume of async API calls; third-party rate limits require throttling |
| 12–18 months | **Reporting Engine** | Report generation is resource-intensive and bursty; dedicated queue and workers |
| 18+ months | **Injury / Medical Service** | Strict HIPAA isolation requirements; dedicated medical compliance team |

### 26.4 Data Architecture

#### 26.4.1 Transactional Data (PostgreSQL)

- **Primary Database:** Multi-tenant PostgreSQL cluster with `organization_id` as the partition key on all tenant-scoped tables.
- **Read Replicas:** 2+ read replicas for dashboard queries, report generation, and audit log searches. Replication lag < 1 second.
- **Connection Pooling:** PgBouncer or RDS Proxy to manage connection limits under scale.
- **Partitioning Strategy:**
  - Time-series tables (DailyLoadSummary, DailyIndexCalculation, WearableSyncRecord) partitioned by `summary_date` (monthly partitions).
  - AuditLog partitioned by `timestamp` (monthly partitions with hot/cold tiering).

#### 26.4.2 Time-Series Data (TimescaleDB)

For high-frequency wearable and GPS data (e.g., 1 Hz heart rate, per-second GPS coordinates):

- **Hypertables:** Automatic chunking by time interval (1 day per chunk).
- **Continuous Aggregates:** Pre-computed rollups (hourly averages, daily summaries) to accelerate dashboard queries.
- **Data Retention:** Raw high-frequency data compressed after 30 days and dropped after 90 days; aggregates retained per organizational policy.

#### 26.4.3 Cache Strategy (Redis)

| Data Type | Cache Key Pattern | TTL | Invalidation |
|-----------|------------------|-----|--------------|
| User Session | `session:{jwt_id}` | 30 min | Logout, role change |
| Dashboard Config | `dashboard:{user_id}` | 24 hours | Config update |
| Team Readiness Grid | `readiness:{team_id}:{date}` | 5 min | Calculation update |
| Athlete Indices | `indices:{athlete_id}:{date}` | 15 min | Recalculation |
| Report Template | `template:{template_id}` | 1 hour | Template update |
| Rate Limit Counter | `ratelimit:{user_id}:{endpoint}` | 1 min | N/A (auto-expire) |

#### 26.4.4 File & Object Storage

- **Hot Tier (S3 Standard):** Reports, uploads, clinical attachments from last 30 days.
- **Warm Tier (S3 Infrequent Access):** Historical reports and exports, 31–365 days.
- **Cold Tier (S3 Glacier / Archive):** Audit log archives, medical imaging backups > 1 year.

### 26.5 Security Architecture

#### 26.5.1 Defense in Depth

| Layer | Control |
|-------|---------|
| **Edge (CDN/WAF)** | Cloudflare/AWS WAF rules for SQL injection, XSS, DDoS mitigation, geo-blocking |
| **Gateway** | Rate limiting (100 req/min per user), API key validation, JWT signature verification |
| **Application** | RBAC enforcement on every endpoint, input validation (Zod/class-validator), parameterized queries |
| **Database** | Row-level security policies (`organization_id` = current tenant), encrypted columns for PII/PHI |
| **Network** | Private subnets for databases and workers; VPC peering for managed services; no public DB endpoints |
| **Data** | AES-256 at rest (TDE for Postgres, S3 SSE); TLS 1.3 in transit; field-level encryption for medical records |

#### 26.5.2 Medical Data Segregation

- **Logical Isolation:** Clinical tables (InjuryDiagnosis, TreatmentNote, MedicalClearance) have additional `medical_access` RBAC checks and separate read replicas if needed.
- **Field-Level Encryption:** Sensitive fields (diagnosis details, clinical notes) encrypted with organization-specific keys before storage.
- **Audit Amplification:** Every access to a medical record generates an audit log entry regardless of read/write.

#### 26.5.3 Secret Management

- **Runtime Secrets:** AWS Secrets Manager / HashiCorp Vault; no environment variables for credentials.
- **Key Rotation:** Database encryption keys and JWT signing keys rotated quarterly with zero-downtime rollover.
- **Development Safety:** Pre-commit hooks block accidental secret commits; `git-secrets` or `truffleHog` scanning in CI.

- **OAuth2 Flow:** User authorizes FIIM to access wearable data; refresh tokens stored encrypted.
- **Polling vs Webhooks:** Polling every 15 minutes for wearables without webhook support; webhook listeners for supported vendors.
- **CSV Fallback:** Every adapter supports manual CSV upload with the same data model mapping if API access is unavailable.

#### 26.6.3 External API (Public API v1 — Post-MVP)

- **Authentication:** API keys with scoped permissions; separate from user JWTs.
- **Rate Limiting:** 1,000 requests/hour per API key; burst allowance for match-day integrations.
- **Webhook Subscriptions:** Customers subscribe to events (alert triggered, athlete readiness updated, injury reported) with HMAC-signed payloads.

#### 26.8.2 Deployment Strategy

- **Blue-Green (Production):** New version deployed to "green" environment; traffic switched via load balancer after health checks pass. Instant rollback by switching back to "blue."
- **Rolling (Staging):** Standard Kubernetes rolling update with maxSurge=1, maxUnavailable=0.
- **Feature Flags:** LaunchDarkly or Unleash for gradual feature activation without redeployment.

#### 26.8.3 Database Migrations

- **Tool:** Flyway or Liquibase; versioned SQL migrations committed to `infra/migrations/`.
- **Process:** Migrations run automatically in CI against staging; production migrations require manual approval and are executed during maintenance windows or blue-green cutover.
- **Rollback:** Every migration has a verified downgrade script; immutable DDL changes only (no `DROP COLUMN` without deprecation cycle).

### 26.9 Monitoring & Observability

#### 26.9.1 The Three Pillars

| Pillar | Tool | Key Metrics / Logs |
|--------|------|--------------------|
| **Metrics** | Prometheus + Grafana | API latency (p50, p95, p99), error rate, throughput, queue depth, cache hit rate, DB connection pool, pod CPU/memory |
| **Logs** | ELK / Datadog Logs | Structured JSON logs with `trace_id`, `organization_id`, `user_id`; searchable by athlete, event type, or error class |
| **Traces** | OpenTelemetry + Jaeger / Datadog APM | End-to-end request tracing across API → Database → Cache → Queue → Worker; identifies bottleneck services |

#### 26.9.2 Alerting Rules (Production)

| Alert Condition | Severity | Response |
|-----------------|----------|----------|
| API p95 latency > 500 ms for 5 min | High | Page on-call engineer |
| Error rate > 1% for 3 min | Critical | Page on-call engineer + Slack channel |
| DB replication lag > 5 seconds | High | Auto-scale read replica; page DBA |
| Queue depth > 1,000 jobs for 10 min | High | Scale worker pods; page platform team |
| JWT signing key expiry < 7 days | Medium | Rotate keys during next maintenance window |
| Certificate expiry < 14 days | Medium | Auto-renew via cert-manager; alert if renewal fails |
| Disk utilization > 80% | High | Trigger cleanup jobs; page platform team |

#### 26.9.3 Health Checks

- **Liveness Probe:** `/health/live` — returns 200 if the process is running. Kubernetes restarts pod if failing.
- **Readiness Probe:** `/health/ready` — returns 200 if DB connection, cache, and critical dependencies are healthy. Traffic routed only to ready pods.
- **Startup Probe:** `/health/startup` — prevents premature liveness/readiness checks during slow initialization (e.g., cache warming).

### 26.10 Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity

#### 26.10.1 RTO / RPO Targets

| Scenario | RTO | RPO | Strategy |
|----------|-----|-----|----------|
| Database failure | < 5 min | < 1 min | Automatic failover to read replica promoted to primary |
| AZ failure | < 10 min | < 5 min | Multi-AZ deployment; Kubernetes reschedules pods to healthy AZ |
| Region failure | < 4 hours | < 5 min | Cross-region read replica + S3 cross-region replication; manual promotion |
| Data corruption | < 4 hours | < 5 min | PITR (point-in-time recovery) to 5-minute granularity |
| Complete platform loss | < 4 hours | < 5 min | Disaster recovery environment in secondary region; Terraform rebuild |

#### 26.10.2 Backup & Recovery Procedures

- **Database:** Automated daily snapshots + continuous WAL archiving to S3. Quarterly restore drills to staging.
- **Object Storage:** Cross-region replication enabled; versioning for accidental deletion recovery.
- **Kubernetes State:** Helm charts and Terraform state stored in version control + S3 backend; recoverable in < 1 hour.
- **Audit Logs:** Real-time streaming to immutable S3 bucket with Object Lock (append-only, no deletion).

#### 26.10.3 Incident Response Playbook

| Severity | Definition | Response Team | Communication |
|----------|------------|---------------|---------------|
| **P1 (Critical)** | Platform unusable, data breach, incorrect risk calculations affecting athletes | All-hands (CEO, CTO, Security) | Customer notification within 1 hour; public status page update |
| **P2 (High)** | Major feature broken, performance severely degraded | Engineering Lead + Platform | Customer notification within 4 hours |
| **P3 (Medium)** | Partial feature degradation, non-critical bugs | Engineering squad | Next business day communication |
| **P4 (Low)** | Cosmetic issues, documentation errors | Engineering squad | Weekly digest |
